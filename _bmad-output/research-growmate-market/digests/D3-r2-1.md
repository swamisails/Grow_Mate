## Claims

29. Allen, Eby & Lentz (2006, JAP 91(3):567-578) is confirmed from the PubMed abstract. In formal programmes, mentors' and protégés' **perceived input into the mentoring process** was consistently related to career mentoring, psychosocial mentoring, role modelling and mentorship quality. The abstract says "input into the mentoring process", not "input into matching". Round-1 summaries that say "input into matching" are an over-specific paraphrase unless the full text says otherwise; the full text was not read. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16737355/ | Journal of Applied Psychology / APA (via PubMed) | 2006-05 (older than 2 years; foundational) | accessed 2026-10-30 | high (peer-reviewed primary abstract) | outcome
30. Same study: **training perceived as high in quality** was consistently related to the same outcomes. The predictor is training *quality*, not simply whether training happened, and the study gives no support for gating mentors by a test. This favours investing in mentor training and participant input over a pass/fail mentor gate. That last step is an inference, because the study did not test gating. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16737355/ | Journal of Applied Psychology / APA (via PubMed) | 2006-05 | accessed 2026-10-30 | high (finding); medium (inference) | outcome

32. **Clutterbuck training statistic, earliest traceable form.** The likely origin is a Clutterbuck Associates article series item (PDF metadata: "CA Article Series 3/11", created 2005-10-20) hosted by the Scottish Mentoring Network. Per the search engine's extract, it says that with only a day or so of training, fewer than one in three relationships deliver significant results, rising to over nine in ten where both mentor and mentee are trained. The PDF text could not be extracted this run, so the wording comes from the search snippet. No underlying sample or method was found. The statistic is the practitioner's own assertion, not a published study. | https://media.scottishmentoringnetwork.co.uk/Whatmakesagoodmentor_6ea9dc8804.pdf | Clutterbuck Associates (hosted by Scottish Mentoring Network) | 2005-10 (dated) | accessed 2026-10-30 | low (practitioner claim, no method) | outcome
33. **The Clutterbuck statistic has drifted as it is repeated.** Training vendors restate it as "only one in three will likely succeed" without training, or "1 in 3 succeed when there is no training", attributed to "Professor David Clutterbuck" with no citation. The 2005 wording is "a day or so of training → <1/3; both trained → >9/10", not "untrained 1/3 vs trained 2/3". The round-1 "1 in 3 vs 2 in 3" form appears to be a distortion. | https://artofmentoring.net/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Mentoring-Training.pdf | Art of Mentoring (vendor) | 2022-02 | accessed 2026-10-30 | low (vendor marketing, unsourced) | outcome
34. **Microsoft certification retake policy:** wait 24 hours after the first failure, then 14 days between later attempts. The cap is 5 attempts per 12 months. Applied Skills *assessment labs* (the closest analogue to an internal skill assessment) need only a 72-hour wait. MOS exams need 24h then 48h, with no annual cap. Waivers are given only for technical failures. | https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/retake-policy | Microsoft Learn | page updated 2026-07-27 | accessed 2026-10-30 | high (official policy) | behavior
35. **AWS certification retake policy:** wait 14 calendar days after a failed exam. Attempts are unlimited, but each costs the full fee. A passed exam cannot be retaken for 2 years. | https://aws.amazon.com/certification/faqs/ | Amazon Web Services | current FAQ (undated) | accessed 2026-10-30 | high (official policy) | behavior
36. **Synthesis (claims 34–35):** major vendor certifications use 24 hours to 14 days between attempts and rely on attempt caps and fees, not long cooldowns, to deter brute-forcing. A flat **one-month** wait before the *first* retake is longer than either Microsoft or AWS. Microsoft's own skills-assessment labs use 72 hours. Google Cloud's help page says only that "the waiting period between attempts varies by exam"; its specific periods were not retrieved. | https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/retake-policy ; https://aws.amazon.com/certification/faqs/ ; https://support.google.com/cloud-certification/answer/9907853 | Microsoft / AWS / Google | 2026 | accessed 2026-10-30 | medium (2 of 4 named vendors verified) | pain
